AI Security Bootcamp · AISB

Frontier AI Security, for people who break things

A 7-day intensive for senior security professionals: offensive and defensive cybersecurity applied to machine-learning systems, with AI-safety concepts taught through a practical red-team lens.

7 days senior practitioners ~70% offense / defense ~30% AI safety

01 LLM fundamentals

How a model actually reads input — and why every layer of that is an attack surface.

Tokenization & chat templates
Models see tokens, not text. A conversation is serialized with special role tokens; attacks abuse that serialization.
Log-probs as an attack surface
Exposed next-token probabilities leak hidden information and training data.
Instruction hierarchy & prefills
System > developer > user ranking; attacks collapse the hierarchy or prefill the assistant's reply.

02 LLM attacks

The practical exploit catalog against deployed language models.

Prompt injection
Malicious instructions hidden in content the model reads hijack it — the top practical LLM vulnerability.
RAG poisoning
Poison the retrieved knowledge base to control the model's answers.
Jailbreaking
Prompts crafted to bypass safety training.
Guardrails
Keyword filters, classifiers, LLM-as-judge, linear probes — build them and bypass them.

03 Stealing & tampering with models

Taking a model apart, copying it, and rewriting what it knows.

Knowledge-distillation attack
Query a model to train a copy — model theft.
Weight extraction via SVD
Reconstruct weights from the model's outputs.
Model editing
Surgically change facts or behaviors directly in the weights.
Backdooring
A hidden trigger causes malicious behavior on demand.
Abliteration / LoRA safety removal
Strip safety training out of an open-weights model.

04 Adversarial ML

Optimization-driven attacks in pixel and embedding space.

Adversarial attacks on vision models
Invisible perturbations fool classifiers.
GCG
Automatically finds a jailbreak suffix string.
Continuous adversarial prefixes
Optimize directly in embedding space (whitebox).
Watermarking / provenance
Detecting AI-generated content and testing watermark robustness.

05 AI Control

Safety-flavored: assume the model might be against you, and contain it anyway.

Premise
Assume the model may be untrustworthy; design the system to contain it.
Protocols
Trusted monitoring, defer-to-trusted, resampling — increasingly central for coding agents.

06 Infrastructure & hardware security

From threat modeling down to bit-flips in GPU memory.

MITRE ATLAS threat modeling
The AI-system counterpart to ATT&CK.
Securing model weights (RAND tiers)
Protect the crown jewels against escalating classes of threat actor.
Side-channel monitoring
Infer hardware activity from power, timing, and EM signals.
GPU RowHammer
Bit-flip hardware attack against GPU memory.
NVIDIA Container Toolkit — CVE-2025-23266
Container-escape vulnerability in GPU tooling.
Agent-swarm incident response
Handling incidents across many autonomous agents at once.
